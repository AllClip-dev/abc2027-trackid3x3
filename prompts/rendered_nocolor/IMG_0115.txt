You are shown {N} frames from a 3x3 basketball game (3 players per team, 6 on the court), in time
order, sampled {INTERVAL} seconds apart. They come from one fixed camera standing beside an outdoor
half-court, looking down at it obliquely, so the whole court is visible. The players are small (often
only 40-70 pixels tall), so look closely.

The six players on the court form two teams of three. The players of one team wear bibs of one colour,
the players of the other team wear bibs of another colour, so you can tell the teams apart by the
bibs. Call the two teams "T1" and "T2" (the labels are arbitrary: name whichever team you meet first
"T1", and keep the same labels for the whole batch). The numbers printed on the bibs are
  - one team: 4, 10, 12
  - the other team: 1, 3, 5
A number can appear in both teams (for example 6 or 9): then the bib colour tells you which team the
player is in. Each player has exactly one number from his team's list.

For every frame, detect a tight bounding box (head to feet) for every player who is ON THE COURT.
There are at most 3 players per team, 6 in total. Ignore everyone else, even when they wear bibs:
the substitutes and the scorekeeper standing or sitting beside the court (near the score board), any
adult in a black shirt (coach, referee, helper), and people far from the court. A player who steps
just over the court line still counts. Do not guess at a box for someone you cannot actually see.

Not every frame shows all six players. A player can be hidden behind another player, cut off by the
edge of the image, or briefly outside the camera view. Return a detection only for a player who is
actually visible in that frame, so a frame can have fewer than 6 detections (5, 4 or even fewer; an
empty list is fine). Never add or duplicate a box just to reach 6, and never give one person two boxes.

Give each distinct player one "person_id" (an integer starting at 1) that stays the same for that
player in every frame of this batch where they appear. Use the bib colour, the number, shorts, shoes,
hair, height and position to decide who is who; players of the same team look alike, so follow each
one from frame to frame by where they are and how they move, also through screens and when two
players overlap. Two people visible in the same frame must never share a person_id. A batch has at
most 6 person_ids.

For each person give "team": "T1" or "T2".

For every detection (one person in one frame) give "jersey": the bib number you can read on that
player IN THAT FRAME, as a string (e.g. "6", "10"). It must be one of the numbers listed above, and
it must be a number of that player's team. Read each frame on its own; do not copy a number from
another frame. Use null if the number is not readable in that frame (facing away, turned sideways,
blurred, occluded, too small). Do not guess.

Return ONLY JSON, no prose, no markdown fences, in exactly this shape:
{"people": [{"person_id": 1, "team": "T1"}, ...],
 "frames": [{"frame": 0,
             "detections": [{"person_id": 1, "jersey": "10", "bbox_1000": [x1, y1, x2, y2]},
                            {"person_id": 2, "jersey": null, "bbox_1000": [x1, y1, x2, y2]}, ...]},
            {"frame": 1, "detections": [...]}, ...]}

bbox_1000 is [x1, y1, x2, y2] normalised to a 0-1000 grid regardless of the actual image size,
top-left origin. "frame" is the 0-based index matching the order the {N} images were attached in
(0 to {N_LAST}). Every one of the {N} frames must appear in "frames", even if its "detections" list
is empty.
